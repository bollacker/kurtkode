# Literature search for the kurtkode cost-optimization audit (2024-2026)

Verification tags used in the "Key finding" column:
- [opened] = I fetched the page (abstract or full text) and the finding is from it.
- [snippet] = finding comes only from a web-search result summary; I did not open the primary page. Treat as less certain.
- "unverified" = a secondary or third-party claim I could not check against the primary source.

Today is 2026-10-26. Several 2026 arXiv items are preprints that are not peer reviewed.

## 1. Cascades, routing, deferral signals, calibration

| Source | Year | URL | Key finding | Implication for kurtkode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FrugalGPT, Chen, Zaharia, Zou (TMLR 2024) | 2023/24 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176 | [snippet] LLM cascade learns which model to try; matches GPT-4 at up to 98% lower cost, or +4% accuracy at equal cost. Cascade needs a learned scorer deciding whether to accept each answer. | Supports the tiered-pool idea in principle. The 98% figure is on QA/classification tasks with cheap answer scorers, not multi-step coding, so do not transfer the number. |
| RouterBench, Hu et al. | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12031 | [opened] 405k+ precomputed inference outcomes for routing evaluation; one-shot prompts (MBPP, GSM8k, MMLU, etc.). A third-party summary claims 2-5x savings (unverified). | Useful template: store (task, model, outcome, cost) tuples so a router can be evaluated offline. Kurtkode's kNN router needs the same dataset. Not agentic. |
| RouteLLM, Ong et al. (ICLR 2025) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665 | [opened] Strong-vs-weak routers trained on preference data cut cost over 2x in some cases without quality loss; routers transfer when the model pair is swapped. | Supports a two-tier decision as the unit of routing. Preference data is chat, not tool-using agent runs. |
| Hybrid LLM, Ding et al. (ICLR 2024) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14618 | [opened] Router predicts query difficulty with a test-time-adjustable quality target; up to 40% fewer large-model calls with no quality drop. | Supports a tunable threshold (a cost/quality dial) over a fixed rule. |
| Language Model Cascades: Token-Level Uncertainty and Beyond, Gupta et al. (ICLR 2024) | 2024 | https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18893 | [snippet] Sequence-level uncertainty is biased by output length; learned post-hoc deferral rules on token-level signals beat simple aggregation; no single simple rule works across tasks. | Warns against a single fixed confidence rule. Needs logprobs, which most OpenRouter/Anthropic routes do not return. |
| LLM Cascades with Mixture of Thought Representations, Yue et al. (ICLR 2024) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03094 | [snippet] Answer consistency of the weak model (mixing CoT and program-of-thought samples) is the deferral signal; GPT-4-level accuracy at about 40% of cost on 6 reasoning benchmarks. | Agreement across samples works for short answers. For agent runs, a "sample twice" check doubles the cheap-tier bill and agreement on diffs is hard to define. |
| Agreement-Based Cascading (ABC), Kolawole et al. (TMLR 2025) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02348 | [snippet] Defer when an ensemble of same-tier models disagrees; no training; claims 2-25x lower API price vs other cascades. | Supports agreement between two cheap models as a trained-free signal. See Kim et al. below: agreement is weaker evidence when models share training lineage. |
| Semantic Agreement Enables Efficient Open-Ended LLM Cascades, Soiffer, Kolawole, Smith (EMNLP 2025 Industry) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21837 | [snippet] Agreement in meaning across ensemble outputs; reaches target-model quality at about 40% of cost, up to 60% less latency; black-box compatible, models 500M-70B. | Best-evidenced training-free deferral signal for free-form output. Still unproven on tool-using coding agents. |
| Correlated Errors in LLMs, Kim et al. (ICML 2025) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962 | [snippet] Across 350+ LLMs, models agree on the same wrong answer about 60% of the time on one leaderboard; correlation grows with shared architecture or developer and with model accuracy. | Supports cross-vendor review (kurtkode's choice). Warns that two cheap models agreeing is weaker evidence than it looks. |
| Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty?, Xiong et al. (ICLR 2024) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063 | [snippet] Verbalized confidence is overconfident (clusters at 80-100%); sampling-consistency beats verbalized in most cases; white-box logprob methods only modestly better (AUROC about 0.52 to 0.61). | Do not trust a cheap model's "I'm 90% sure". If a self-signal is used, consistency across samples beats the stated number. |
| Just Ask for Calibration, Tian et al. (EMNLP 2023) | 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975 | [snippet] For RLHF models, verbalized confidence is often better calibrated than token probabilities (ECE roughly halved); Llama-2-70B-chat mixed; tested on factual QA only. | Partly contradicts Xiong: verbalized confidence can beat logprobs on RLHF models, but only measured on factual recall. Measure per model on your own tasks. |
| Do Small Language Models Know When They're Wrong?, Burleigh | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19781 | [opened] Verbalized-confidence cascades on 2,100 scored items: best small model AUROC 0.857; one small model produced a near-degenerate confidence distribution and could not close the gap at any threshold; best cascade -76% cost at kappa 0.802 vs 0.819. | Per-model calibration varies from useful to useless. Tier-specific calibration check before using any self-confidence signal (ling-3.0-flash and deepseek-v4-flash may differ). |
| Uncertainty Quantification for LLM Agents, Lin et al. | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07395 | [opened] Step-level calibration does not imply trajectory-level calibration; confidence taken from the agent's own responses does not consistently beat a simple baseline; averaging hides late-stage overconfidence. | Agent self-reported confidence is a poor deferral signal. Supports kurtkode's choice to rely on grounded checks and external judges rather than self-report. |
| Can an Agent Learn to Predict Its Own Failure?, Hathidara | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614 | [opened] Untrained 7B agents' step confidence is near chance for episode outcome; RL on outcome-grounded rewards lifts AUROC to 0.68-0.85 (ALFWorld, TextWorld, WebShop; not coding). | Self-prediction needs training; not available off the shelf for kurtkode's models. |
| Conformal Cascade, Dou, Lian, Li | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25018 | [opened] Defer when the conformal prediction set is not a singleton; distribution-free coverage guarantee 1 - K*alpha on 18 multiple-choice benchmarks. | Theory-backed thresholding, but MCQ only. Relevant idea: calibrate a threshold on held-out outcomes instead of hand-setting it. |
| LLMRouterBench, Li et al. (Findings of ACL 2026) | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07206 | [opened] 400K+ instances, 21 datasets, 33 models, 10 routers. Many routers perform alike; several recent ones, including a commercial router, do not reliably beat the best single model; big remaining gap to oracle from model-recall failures; embedding backbone matters little; careful model curation beats larger ensembles. | Strong caution: a router must beat "always use the best single model" to count. Supports a small curated pool. Embedding choice for the kNN router is a minor lever. |
| Rethinking Predictive LLM Routing: When Simple kNN Beats Complex Learned Routers, Li | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12601 | [opened abstract] A well-tuned kNN over query embeddings matches or beats learned routers (MLP, GNN) with lower sample complexity. A search snippet gives numbers (AUC 52.68 kNN vs 51.71 MLP) that I did not verify. | Directly supports kurtkode's planned kNN router. Margins are small, so expect little over a trivial baseline. |
| Dynamic Model Routing and Cascading for Efficient LLM Inference: A Survey, Moslem and Kelleher | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04445 | [snippet] Distinguishes routing (pick one model) from cascading (try cheap, escalate on a quality estimate); systems often combine both. | Useful vocabulary: kurtkode is a hybrid (pre-routing by importance, post-hoc ladder). |
| SWE-Router, Son et al. | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00053v1 | [opened] On SWE-bench Verified, letting the cheap model run K=1-4 turns then scoring the partial trajectory with a fine-tuned value head (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B) raises route-AUC from about 0.55-0.63 (prompt-only) to about 0.70-0.78. Escalated runs restart from the original prompt (continuing from the weak trajectory biased the strong model toward its mistakes); weak-model turns still count toward cost. | Strongest evidence for kurtkode's retry-one-rung-up: restart cleanly, and count the sunk cost. Also supports deferring the decision until after a short cheap exploration, not before. |
| Scrouting, Bhola et al. (SuperAGI) | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04804 | [opened] Solve sets are largely nested (containment 0.94 Verified, 0.91 Multilingual, 0.77 Pro), so no learned router beat "always the strongest"; accuracy routing has little headroom, cost routing is where gains are. 249 of 266 handoffs claimed a verified reproduction; only 50 were real. A 7B scout plus cheap fixer matched Opus 4.6 at about 18% of cost per solve. | Two points. (1) Gains come from cost, as in kurtkode's goal. (2) Agent claims of verification are mostly false (70% here), which validates the "no report" and "re-run the verify check" grounded checks. One benchmark slice, one-task margin. |
| vLLM Semantic Router issue 5374, "When does restarting a coding agent's task on a stronger model pay?" | 2026 | https://github.com/vllm-project/semantic-router/issues/5374 | [opened] Practitioner research note on recorded Terminal-Bench 2.0 and SWE runs: zero-shot judge AUROC for predicting cheap-attempt failure 0.68-0.86 on Terminal-Bench, 0.67-0.76 on SWE; instruction-only judging 0.59-0.65; escalation gain over best single model is positive for deepseek-v3.2 to Opus 4.6 (+0.10 to +0.16) but intervals include zero, negative for gpt-5-mini to Opus; online-learned thresholds often lost. Conclusion: escalation pays only in a narrow band. Not peer reviewed. | Closest match to kurtkode's judge-then-escalate design. Supports keeping escalation advisory and learning thresholds only from outcomes you pay to observe. Warns that a mediocre judge plus a small price gap makes automatic escalation net negative. |
| TwinRouterBench, Yang et al. | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18859 | [opened] Agentic routing benchmark: 970 router-visible prefixes with execution-verified target tiers (static) plus a live 500-case SWE-bench Verified track scored by resolution and real spend. Abstract reports no router results. | A template for evaluating a per-call router on resolved-task and spend, not prompt quality. |
| TACIT-Switch, Lei and Huang | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27911 | [opened] Learns permanent handoff policies from interval-censored teacher annotations; +7.4 to +11.1 points over task-level, step-level, fixed-prefix baselines in simulation; best held-out success among learned policies on ALFWorld and DABench. | Research direction: handoff timing from accumulated trajectory risk. Not coding-agent evidence; mostly simulation. |
| CodeRescue (arXiv 2607.19338) | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19338 | [opened] A router choosing cheap recovery (reflect/replan) vs escalation reported exceeding always-escalate solve rate at 35% of its recovery cost (GPT-5.4-nano to GPT-5.4). WITHDRAWN at the authors' institution's request (v2 is a withdrawal notice). | Do not rely on it. Listed only because it appears in searches; the idea "try cheap recovery before escalating" is untested here. |

## 2. LLM-as-judge

| Source | Year | URL | Key finding | Implication for kurtkode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena, Zheng et al. (NeurIPS 2023) | 2023 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685 | [snippet] GPT-4 judge agrees with humans over 80%; documents position, verbosity, and self-enhancement bias; swapping order exposes inconsistency. | Baseline for judge bias. Grader path should swap order or use pointwise rubrics and not be the same family as the author. |
| LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations, Panickssery et al. (NeurIPS 2024) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076 | [snippet] Self-preference scales linearly with self-recognition after fine-tuning; off-the-shelf GPT-4 and Llama 2 recognize their own text above chance. | Supports cross-vendor review and a grader from a different family than the worker. |
| JudgeBench, Tan et al. (ICLR 2025) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784 | [snippet] On hard pairs with objective correctness labels (knowledge, reasoning, math, coding), strong judges like GPT-4o score only slightly above chance (about 51% in the arXiv table). Newer reasoning judges do much better (aggregator figure 80.9% for o3-mini, unverified). | Judges are weakest exactly where cheap models are wrong in subtle ways. A cheap grader on hard items is near coin-flip; use a reasoning-tier grader or ground truth. |
| CodeJudge, Tong and Zhang (EMNLP 2024) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02184 | [snippet] Execution-free "analyze then summarize" judging with fault taxonomy beats prior LLM judges across 4 datasets, 5 languages; an 8B judge beat a GPT-3.5 baseline. | Structured, step-by-step judging for code helps. Supports rubric-style grader prompts over a single yes/no. |
| CodeJudgeBench, Jiang et al. | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10535 | [opened] 26 judges on code generation, repair, unit-test generation; thinking models clearly beat non-thinking, Qwen3-8B-thinking beats judge-tuned models up to 70B; order swaps change accuracy; pairwise beats scalar scoring. A snippet says swaps move accuracy up to 14 points (not in the abstract; unverified). | Judge reasoning effort matters more than judge size. Pairwise comparison is more reliable than 1-10 scores. |
| Bias in the Loop: Auditing LLM-as-a-Judge for Software Engineering, Zhao, Esmaeili, Fard | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16790v1 | [opened] 12 prompt-injected biases (position, authority, verbosity, self-enhancement, sentiment, etc.) on code judges; small judges (Qwen2.5-Coder-3B) show huge position effects (92.6% vs 8.4% accuracy by answer position under one bias); high consistency does not mean correctness. | Small/cheap judges are badly biased. Trigger execution (compile, tests) when judge verdicts are unstable. Report bias sensitivity. |
| Agent-as-a-Judge, Zhuge et al. (ICML 2025) | 2024/25 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934 | [snippet] Judging agent trajectories with an agentic judge: about 90% agreement with expert consensus on DevAI vs about 70% for plain LLM-as-judge; cost saving is against humans, and cost was roughly equal to a plain LLM judge but 10x slower (secondary source). | Supports a judge that can read files (the "parent path") over a no-tools grader for process judgments. |
| AgentRewardBench, Lù et al. | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08942 | [snippet] 1,302 web-agent trajectories labeled by experts; no single LLM judge excels across benchmarks; rule-based evaluation underreports success. A summary claims about 70% best precision (unverified). | Judges for agents are mediocre and benchmark-specific. Calibrate the kurtkode judges on labeled kurtkode trajectories before trusting a threshold. |
| G-Eval (Liu et al. 2023) and Prometheus (Kim et al., ICLR 2024) | 2023/24 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08491 | [snippet] Rubric plus chain-of-thought judging improves human alignment; a 13B Prometheus matches GPT-4 judge (Pearson 0.897 vs 0.882) when given a reference answer and rubric. G-Eval flags bias toward LLM-written text. | Rubric and reference-answer judging allows a cheap judge to work, but only with references. Tasks with a verify command give you a reference for free. |
| LLM-as-a-Judge for Software Engineering: Literature Review, He et al. | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24367 | [opened metadata only; PDF text unreadable] Survey of judge use in SE. A search snippet cited Crupi et al.: smaller/open models more often label incorrect code as correct (unverified). | Context only. |

## 3. Self-verification, test generation, execution-based checks

| Source | Year | URL | Key finding | Implication for kurtkode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet, Huang et al. (ICLR 2024) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798 | [snippet] Without external feedback, self-correction does not help and sometimes hurts; reported gains relied on oracle labels. | Supports using external grounded signals rather than asking the same cheap model to double-check. |
| When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes?, Kamoi et al. (TACL 2024) | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297 | [snippet] No prior work shows successful self-correction from prompted-LLM feedback alone (outside favorable tasks); it works with reliable external feedback or fine-tuning. | Same: "re-run the verify check" (reliable feedback) is the right kind of signal; an LLM retrying itself is not. |
| R2E-Gym, Jain et al. (NeurIPS 2025) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164 | [snippet] Execution-based (generated tests) and execution-free (LLM) verifiers each plateau around 42-43% on SWE-bench Verified best-of-N; hybrid reaches 51%. Test verifiers have low distinguishability; LLM verifiers rely on style. | Supports combining free execution checks with an LLM judge rather than choosing one. |
| SWE-Gym, Pan et al. (ICML 2025) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139 | [snippet] Verifiers trained on agent trajectories give roughly log-linear gains with number of samples; 32.0% on Verified with open-weight agent. | Trained verifiers help but need training data; not an off-the-shelf option. |
| Large Language Monkeys, Brown et al. | 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787 | [snippet] On SWE-bench Lite, 250 samples of DeepSeek-Coder-V2 solve 56% vs 15.9% with one sample, beating a 43% single-attempt frontier baseline; five cheap samples cost less than one frontier sample; gains need an automatic verifier. | Alternative to escalation: when a task has a trustworthy verify command, repeated cheap attempts may beat one expensive one. Without a verifier the approach stalls. |
| The Limits of Inference Scaling Through Resampling, Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan (ICLR 2026) | 2024/26 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501 | [snippet] With imperfect verifiers (weak unit tests), false positives cap accuracy no matter the budget; weaker models match stronger ones on weak tests but fail extended tests; optimal sample count often under 10. | Warns that "verify command passes" is a leaky detector; cheap-model passes may be false positives. Keep a second, independent check. |
| Are "Solved Issues" in SWE-bench Really Solved Correctly?, Wang, Pradel, Liu (ICSE 2026) | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223 | [snippet] Weak validation inflates SWE-bench Verified resolution by 6.2 points; 29.6% of plausible patches behave differently from ground truth, 28.6% of those certainly wrong. | The task-level verify check misses behavior changes. Supports the diff review and "files the request did not name" checks as complements. |
| Rethinking the Value of Agent-Generated Tests, Chen et al. | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07900 | [opened] Agent-written tests are mostly print-style probes; resolved and unresolved tasks show similar test-writing rates; prompting more or fewer tests did not significantly change outcomes. | Do not ask cheap agents to write tests as a failure detector; use pre-existing or user-supplied checks. |
| Knowing When to Stop: The Evidence-Action Gap in Coding Agents (ImpasseBench), Chen et al. | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31895 | [opened] 8 frontier models, 19,371 trajectories: agents over-persist and report success their logs do not support; an escalate-to-human tool cut budget exhaustion from 34.0% to 6.7%; escalation tickets omitted the agent's progress and verification status. | Supports an escalation affordance. The advisory card should carry progress and verification status. Also supports the "no report / error stop" checks. |

## 4. Cost of agentic coding, caching, multi-agent, context

| Source | Year | URL | Key finding | Implication for kurtkode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anthropic prompt caching docs | 2026 (live) | https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching | [opened] 5-minute write 1.25x, 1-hour write 2x, reads 0.1x base input (0.05x on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5; 0.025x on Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1). | How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money?, Bai et al. | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750 | [opened abstract] 8 frontier models on SWE-bench Verified: agentic coding uses about 1000x the tokens of code chat; input tokens dominate cost even with caching; same-task runs differ up to 30x in tokens; accuracy often peaks at intermediate cost; models underpredict their own usage (correlation up to 0.39). | Confirms the team's "input tokens dominate" lesson. | Effective context engineering for AI agents, Anthropic | 2025 | https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents | [opened] Context is a finite attention budget; compaction and clearing old tool results; subagents return a 1,000-2,000 token summary after exploring tens of thousands. | Supports the delegation nudge design. No benchmark numbers given. |
| An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents, Fan et al. | 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804 | [opened] 4 models, SWE-Bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1, 176 settings: rule-based elision before LLM summarization is the most efficient context strategy; planning is an accuracy scaffold for weak models and a cost saver for strong ones; bash-only interface cheaper for bash-capable models. | Prefer cheap context pruning (drop old tool output) to LLM summarization; planning step is worth more for cheap models. |
| Frontier AI at a fraction of the cost: open worker with closed advisor, Fireworks | 2026 | https://fireworks.ai/blog/frontier-open-source-worker-with-closed-source-advisor | [opened] Vendor blog. Open worker (Kimi-K2.6, GLM-5.2) plus one read-only Opus 4.8 review call at the end: SWE-bench Pro 55-59% to 59-66%; cost $6.1 vs $18.28 for Opus alone; plan-plus-review no better than review-only; the worker reviewing itself gave little or no lift. 60-100 tasks per cell, so +-1-2 points noise. | Closest published analogue to an "advisory escalation card": one strong-model review at the end, not a rerun. Suggests the strong tier's best use is as a reviewer, and a cheap reviewer is insufficient. |
| SWE-in-a-team, letsship.ai | 2026 | https://letsship.ai/blog/swe-in-a-team | [opened] Blog benchmark, 20 tickets, 13 builder configurations, planner/reviewer/tester pinned to Opus 4.8: cheapest builder to resolve 20/20 was deepseek-v4-flash at $3.16 per resolved ticket vs Opus 5 at $5.48; one trial each; directional only. | Matches the team's finding that cheap models pass these tasks. Also shows fixed expensive roles can dominate the bill, which makes the cheap tier's saving small. |
| SWR-Bench and AI code review accuracy | 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01494 | [snippet] Best LLM code-review F1 was 19.38%; precision low (other techniques under 10%); an industry study reported 33.9% precision, 20% recall. Vendor false-discovery figures vary 6%-82% (unverified). | A cheap model reviewing a diff will be noisy: treat its findings as a trigger for a free check, not a verdict. |

## Synthesis

**Where the evidence supports kurtkode.** Preferring grounded, free checks over self-report is the best-supported choice. Self-correction without external feedback does not work (Huang; Kamoi), agent self-confidence is not reliably better than a naive baseline (Lin et al.) and is near chance untrained (Hathidara), and coding agents falsely claim verification most of the time (Scrouting: 70% of claimed reproductions were fake; Evidence-Action Gap). "No report", "error stop" and "re-run the verify check" are exactly the signals this literature favors. Restarting cleanly on the next rung is supported by SWE-Router (continuing from a weak trajectory biases the strong model), and cross-vendor review is supported by correlated-error and self-preference results. Input-token dominance, context rot, and read-heavy exploration (about 76% of tokens) back the delegation nudge and a cheap exploration tier. A kNN router is supported (Li 2025), and static task features predict difficulty (AUC 0.863).

**Which cheap deferral signals have the best evidence.** (1) Execution outcomes (tests, build, verify command), which beat every LLM signal but leak when tests are weak (6.2-point inflation; resampling ceilings). (2) Semantic agreement across independent runs or models (ABC, Semantic Agreement; about 40% of cost), best for free-form output but weakened by correlated errors and unproven on tool-using coding agents. (3) Post-hoc judges on the finished attempt, with measured AUROC about 0.68-0.86 (vLLM note) and 0.74-0.76 on SWE; judges on the task text alone are near 0.6. (4) Partial-trajectory value models (SWE-Router) beat prompt-only routing by 12-15 points, but need training. Verbalized confidence is model-specific: useful for one small model (AUROC 0.857), degenerate for another. Logprobs are rarely available.

**Does automatic escalation pay?** Only in a narrow band. The vLLM note finds gains with intervals including zero, negative for a gpt-5-mini to Opus pair, and online-learned thresholds that lost money at two of four price points. Scrouting finds solve sets nested, so accuracy routing has little headroom and the money is in cost. Kurtkode's choice to keep escalation advisory is consistent with this. The one strong positive result is structural: a cheap worker plus a single strong read-only review at the end (Fireworks, vendor data, noisy) cut cost about two-thirds, and a cheap model reviewing itself did nothing. That suggests the escalation card should request a strong-tier review of the final diff and verification status, not a rerun.

**Where it contradicts or suggests change.** (a) A cheap cross-vendor diff reviewer will have low precision (code-review F1 about 19%); gate it behind a free check. (b) A judge's value depends on a price gap and on its AUROC on your own models, so measure both per tier pair before enabling anything automatic. (c) Mixed-model sessions pay cache-rebuild costs; Cursor says offline evals ignore this, and a 64% cache share against Claude Code's 91% example is worth auditing. (d) Saturated benchmarks cannot validate any of this; DeepSWE-style original tasks, implementation-agnostic verifiers, several trials per cell (30x run variance), and paired comparisons are needed. (e) Current practice (Claude Code, Codex, opencode, Aider) is manual per-role model choice, not automatic escalation; Cursor is the exception, with a pre-task classifier trained on 600k requests.

Caveats: most 2026 items are preprints or vendor blogs; several pages were abstract-only or search-snippet-only (tagged in the tables).
